🎬 A Brief History of Horror Cinema


📦 1. The Silent Era & Gothic Roots (1890s–1920s)

The roots of horror cinema trace back to the late 19th century with Georges Méliès's early illusions. However, the genre truly took shape during the 1920s through German Expressionism. Masterpieces like The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920) and Nosferatu (1922) introduced highly stylized, distorted sets and heavy shadows. These techniques perfectly captured psychological dread and set the visual blueprint for all gothic horror to come.

🧟 2. Universal Monsters & The Golden Age (1930s–1950s)

In the 1930s, Universal Pictures revolutionized the genre by bringing gothic literature to the silver screen. Characters like Dracula (1931) and Frankenstein (1931), famously portrayed by Bela Lugosi and Boris Karloff, became cultural icons. By the 1950s, the horror landscape shifted from supernatural monsters to Sci-Fi horror and creature features, fueled by Cold War anxieties and atomic age fears, resulting in classics like The Thing from Another World.

🔪 3. Psycho-Thrillers & The Slasher Boom (1960s–1980s)

In 1960, Alfred Hitchcock’s Psycho shocked the world by moving the monster from crumbling castles into everyday motels. The late 1960s and 1970s brought raw, visceral terror with The Exorcist (1973) and The Texas Chain Saw Massacre (1974). This paved the way for the golden era of slasher films in the 1980s, dominated by unstoppable antagonists in franchises like Halloween, Friday the 13th, and A Nightmare on Elm Street.
