A Comprehensive Analysis of Horror Film Genres (Part B)

Explore the deepest fears within the human psyche and the inexplicable supernatural forces.

3. Slasher horror

“When a mysterious phone call turns a horror movie trivia game into a real-life, bloody survival nightmare...”

Slasher horror is a classic horror subgenre centered on a usually masked, relentless killer armed with bladed weapons. The killer stalks and kills a group of isolated victims one after another. It often features young characters, rising body counts, suspenseful chase scenes, and the well-known “final girl” trope, where one survivor confronts the killer at the climax. Famous examples include Halloween and Scream.

Classic Representative Work: Scream

4. Folk/body Horror

“When a cursed videotape and a simple phone call seal an inescapable, seven-day death sentence...”

Folk body horror mixes folk horror and body horror. It ties grotesque bodily mutation, decay or distortion to old rural legends, pagan rituals, local curses and ancient folk beliefs. The body is twisted and violated not by a simple human killer, but by old magic, cult ceremonies or forces from the land. It creates a heavy sense of primitive dread. Midsommar is a typical reference work.

Classic Representative Work: The Ring
